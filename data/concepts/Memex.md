# Memex

A hypothetical personal knowledge-storage device proposed by [[Vannevar Bush]] in his 1945 essay *As We May Think*. The Memex was envisioned as a desk-sized machine storing books, records, and personal notes on microfilm. Its defining feature was **associative trails** — the ability to link arbitrary documents into navigable sequences that reflect the user's own patterns of thought.

## Key Properties

- **Personal and private** — your own curated store, not a public network.
- **Actively curated** — the user builds trails between documents deliberately.
- **Associative linking first-class** — the connections between documents are as valuable as the documents themselves.

## Contrast with the Web

Bush's vision diverged from what the World Wide Web became. The web is public, broadcast-oriented, and largely uncurated. Memex was intended to be private and deeply curated, with associative trails serving as durable personal artifacts.

## Relation to [[LLM Wiki Pattern]]

The LLM Wiki pattern is a direct descendant in spirit. Bush couldn't solve one problem: who does the bookkeeping of building and maintaining those trails? Humans abandon it. LLMs do it at near-zero cost.
